Honeybee

Contents

  • 1 Life in the hive
  • 2 Communication

A honeybee is a social insect that lives in large colonies and makes honey from the nectar of flowers.[1] A single colony can contain tens of thousands of workers, a few hundred males and one queen.

Life in the hive[edit]

Worker bees change jobs as they grow older. Young workers clean the cells and feed the larvae, while older workers build wax, guard the entrance and finally leave the hive to collect food.

Communication[edit]

Bees tell each other where to find food with a movement known as the waggle dance. The angle of the dance shows the direction of the flowers compared with the sun, and its length shows how far away they are.

Scientists still study the dance today, because it is one of the few known examples of an animal sharing exact information about distant places.
