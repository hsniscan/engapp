How to read a train timetable

Train timetables look complicated at first, but they follow a few simple rules. Each column is one train and each row is one station, so you read down a column to follow a single journey from start to finish.

Times in the table are usually given in the twenty-four hour format. A train that leaves at 17:45 leaves at a quarter to six in the evening. If a station has no time in a column, that train does not stop there.

Small letters next to a time are footnotes. They might tell you that the train runs only on weekdays, that you must change at the next station, or that there is a cafe on board. Always read the notes at the bottom of the page.

Finally, check the date at the top of the timetable. Timetables change in spring and in winter, and an old one can easily make you miss the last train home.
