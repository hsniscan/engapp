What I learned from a year of growing tomatoes on a balcony

Posted by Sam Whitfield

When I moved into a small flat last winter, I was sure that my gardening days were over. The balcony was narrow, it faced west, and the wind in the afternoons was strong enough to knock over a chair.

Still, I bought four large pots, a bag of compost and six young tomato plants from the market. The first lesson came quickly: small pots dry out in a few hours on a hot day, so I had to water every morning and sometimes again in the evening.

The second lesson was about support. Tomatoes grow tall and heavy, and the wind kept breaking the stems. Tying each plant to a strong stick and to the railing solved most of the problem.

By August I was picking a bowl of tomatoes every other day. They were not perfect, but they tasted far better than anything from the supermarket, and my neighbours were happy to take the extra ones.

If you have a balcony and a little patience, try it. Start with two plants, choose the biggest pots you can carry, and do not forget to water them.
